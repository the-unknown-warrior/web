One photo a day. A life you can actually look back on.

Photo Journal only asks one thing: a single picture, once a day. No captions to write, no streak to keep beyond that. String enough days together and it becomes something a journal rarely manages — a visual autobiography you didn't have to sit down and write, and one day, a way for the people who loved you to see how you actually lived.

The idea

A single frame is easier to keep than a paragraph.

Most journaling fails for the same reason: the blank page. Photo Journal removes it. Once a day, open the app, take or pick one photo, done. It doesn't ask why the day mattered — most days you won't know until years later, looking back.

Every entry drops into a running timeline, one frame per day, in order. Scroll back a year from now and you're not reading notes about your life — you're looking at it. It's the raw material for an autobiography you never had to write a first draft of.

How it works

Three habits, not a hundred

The whole app is small on purpose — the archive is the point, not the features.

01

Capture

Once a day, the app nudges you. Snap a new photo or pick one already on your camera roll — whatever the day actually looked like.

02

Collect

Each entry lines up on a single timeline, oldest to newest. No feed, no likes, no algorithm — just the order you actually lived them in.

03

Keep

Your archive exports as a file you own outright, so it survives app updates, phone changes, and eventually, you — a private legacy, not a platform's growth metric.
